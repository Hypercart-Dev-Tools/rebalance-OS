## Reusable ideas from the strongest candidates

If your goal is to **reuse or fork** rather than merely learn, I would not start from a single repository. I would compose ideas from several.

Onyx is the clearest source for the **synthesis layer**. It already treats many external systems as indexable context, supports deep research and actions, and is comfortable in a “self-hosted AI platform” shape. If you need a dashboard plus agent/action layer quickly, Onyx is the repo most likely to save you months on connectors, auth, indexing, action invocation, and prompt/tool orchestration. The missing piece is your recommendation logic, since Onyx is built to answer and act, not to maintain a first-class “dropped ball” ranker. citeturn32view2turn0search3turn12view1

DevLake and GrimoireLab are the clearest sources for the **data plane**. DevLake’s active plugin system, incremental GitHub collection, and new Tempo timesheets plugin are especially relevant if you want to ingest what teams planned, what they actually shipped, and where work is drifting. GrimoireLab is older in feel, but its retrieve-store-enrich-analyze pipeline and its identity-resolution ecosystem are exactly the kind of thinking you need if the same human appears as a GitHub author, a calendar owner, a mail sender, and a Slack participant across multiple systems. citeturn33view0turn38view1turn21view3turn24search2

Khoj and LifeOS are the best places to study the **local context assembly** side. Khoj shows how an operator-facing “AI second brain” can blend self-hosting, Obsidian-style knowledge, automations, and agent customization. LifeOS, although much smaller, is almost a miniature reference implementation for “personal work context assembled locally, then summarized through MCP.” Its `briefing_server`, local `data/` directory, Google auth reuse, and thin frontend are unusually close to the system shape you described. citeturn36view0turn0search5turn30view0turn33view4

TAMON and Leon are where I would look for the **outer shell** around the recommender. TAMON’s prompt assembly is particularly strong: it explicitly constructs each invocation from identity, integration status, channel context, and task state, then isolates execution with queueing, process cleanup, and worktrees. Leon gives you a more general tool/memory/skills architecture. Neither gives you the prioritization model you want, but both give you patterns for turning a score into a controllable agentic surface. citeturn33view5turn17view0turn21view5

For **component-level reuse**, two smaller MCP repos are worth attention even though they are not full-system matches. [taylorwilsdon/google_workspace_mcp](https://github.com/taylorwilsdon/google_workspace_mcp) is an MIT-licensed Google Workspace MCP server and CLI that exposes Gmail, Calendar, Tasks, Keep, Docs, Sheets, Chat, Drive, Contacts, and Search; GitHub showed 2.7k stars and activity about two weeks before I checked it. [j3k0/mcp-google-workspace](https://github.com/j3k0/mcp-google-workspace) is much smaller, but it is a good example of security-conscious adapter design: recent commits hardened OAuth scopes, added a draft-only mode, and sandboxed Gmail attachment writes. If your system’s privacy stance matters, that narrower repo is instructive even if it is not feature-rich. citeturn8search8turn13view4turn20view0

The most important gap is still yours to build. Across the strongest candidates, I did **not** find documented support for the tunable weighting model you described: per-person trust, per-source weights, redundancy penalties against the operator’s own activity, vagueness discounts, or explicit “about-to-be-dropped” sensitivity. I also did not find a mature OSS repo that explicitly distinguishes a teammate signal that merely echoes what the operator already knows from one that fills a blind spot. That is the clearest whitespace in the field I reviewed. citeturn32view2turn37view0turn36view0turn30view0turn17view0turn21view3

## Verdict

This looks **largely whitespace as a product concept, but not as a component library problem**. I did not find an active open-source repository that is a true near-match for: local-first, multi-person work-signal ingestion into a local store; one ranked “what should we do next?” recommendation; explicit stale/delegated-work detection; redundant-versus-net-new signal handling; privacy-default teammate controls; and MCP/agent exposure all in one system. Among the repos I checked, none strongly documents the scoring semantics that seem central to your idea. citeturn32view2turn37view0turn36view0turn30view0turn17view0turn21view3

If I had to study only three repositories first, I would pick [onyx-dot-app/onyx](https://github.com/onyx-dot-app/onyx), [apache/devlake](https://github.com/apache/devlake), and [khoj-ai/khoj](https://github.com/khoj-ai/khoj). Onyx is the best match for the **LLM synthesis and action surface**; DevLake is the best match for **team ingestion, normalization, and incremental sync**; and Khoj is the best mature match for the **local/self-hosted personal context layer** around documents, notes, agents, and automations. If you care more about a minimal local MCP architecture than maturity, I would swap in [mplanav/LifeOS](https://github.com/mplanav/LifeOS) as a fourth repo to read closely. citeturn32view2turn38view0turn37view0turn38view1turn36view0turn30view0

The closest partial overlaps, and what each is still missing, are straightforward. Onyx is missing the explicit **priority engine** and **blind-spot versus redundancy model**. DevLake is missing the **LLM recommendation layer**, **local-first privacy posture**, and **calendar/email/note integration shape** you want. Khoj is missing the **team dimension** and the **work-ranking semantics**. LifeOS is missing the **GitHub/Slack/team** half of the graph. TAMON is missing the **local analytics store and scorer**. That combination strongly suggests you are not in pure greenfield territory, but you are also not looking at an obvious fork-and-ship situation. The reusable prior art is real; the actual product thesis still looks mostly open. citeturn32view2turn37view0turn36view0turn30view0turn17view0
